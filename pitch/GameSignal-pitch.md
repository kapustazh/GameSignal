# GameSignal pitch

Ten slides, approximately two minutes at 134 words per minute. The timings are rehearsal targets.

## 1. GameSignal

8 seconds

We’re GameSignal. We help game developers decide what to do next during development and after release.

## 2. The problem

14 seconds

AI can help a team build a game. But the team still has to interpret feedback, prepare a release, and find its audience. That decision gap is our problem hypothesis.

## 3. A crowded release environment

8 seconds

SteamDB lists more than twenty-one thousand game releases in 2025. That shows a crowded release environment. Our customer segment still needs validation.

## 4. Our first proposed customer

10 seconds

Our first proposed customers are small game studios facing decisions during development or after release, especially teams without dedicated marketing or publishing support.

## 5. The product

14 seconds

A developer brings their game, goals, constraints, and feedback. GameSignal would analyze that context, recommend existing tools or providers, and propose a clear next action for the team to review.

## 6. Four workflows

12 seconds

Our scope covers four workflows: player feedback, launch planning, advertising preparation, and audience outreach. Each should lead to a useful decision and relevant existing solutions.

## 7. An example after release

14 seconds

For example, player reviews might reveal confusing onboarding. We would summarize that pattern, recommend an improvement and supporting tools, then help the team learn from the next playtest. This example is illustrative.

## 8. Relevant partners

12 seconds

We could also help studios prepare a clear project brief and research publishers or specialist providers that fit their genre, platform, and stage. These connections are proposed.

## 9. Business potential

14 seconds

We would first test paid analysis packages. Disclosed provider referrals could become another revenue stream. Our next step is a small pilot that measures recommendation usefulness and willingness to pay.

## 10. Why We

14 seconds

We’re Matvii, Daniil, and Taras, students at 42. We bring a developer’s perspective and have already turned our discussion into a working concept website. We’re looking for feedback to shape the first pilot.

## Q&A backup

### What is available today?

A live landing page, illustrative workflows and this pitch. AI integrations and service delivery are proposed.

### Why would developers choose this over a general AI assistant?

The hypothesis is that recommendations tied to project stage, feedback and constraints, with clear next actions and provider handoffs, will be more useful. The pilot must test that.

### Who pays?

Small studios are the proposed buyer for analysis packages. Referral revenue is another hypothesis, with disclosed commercial relationships.

### Do you already have publisher partnerships?

No partnerships are established. We propose publisher research and matching before validating introductions with willing partners.

### How would you measure the pilot?

Recommendation usefulness, completion of suggested actions, repeat use and willingness to pay. Establish a baseline before claiming time savings or better commercial outcomes.

### Where did the number come from?

SteamDB’s 2025 release count. We show a rounded 21,000+. It describes platform release volume, and does not establish the number of potential customers.

### What feedback shaped this pitch?

The founding discussion prioritized guidance during development and after release, existing tools and providers, publisher discovery, and a clear concept-stage scope. External developer feedback remains to be collected.
